This week's news came from the Armory Show again. Beyond confirming its dates and exhibitor list, the fair announced its first-ever museum acquisition prizes this week, something it had never done in thirty years of history. There are two. The Pérez Art Museum Miami, the city's contemporary art museum, is putting up twenty thousand dollars to buy a work from the Focus section, the one curated by María Elena Ortiz around the Caribbean, adding it to both its own collection and that of the Green Family Foundation Caribbean Cultural Institute, the museum's affiliated institute dedicated to Caribbean art. The Bronx Museum, the borough's public museum focused on artists from communities underrepresented in the mainstream circuit, is putting up the second prize, fifteen thousand dollars, to buy a work from the Presents section, where fifty five young galleries are showing. Both prizes target artists early in their careers, and both pick the winning work the same weekend as the fair, so whoever wins walks away with a confirmed sale and the validation of entering a museum's permanent collection.

Today's move is to let two or three clients who follow young galleries or Caribbean artists know that the Armory Show is going to reward museum purchases for the first time in its history, and that this makes it even more worth paying close attention to the Focus and Presents sections when the fair opens on September the twenty fourth. A museum prize does not move a work's price on its own, but it does add a line of institutional provenance that can later help justify any future sale. If any of your clients know an artist who might fit into either section, give them a heads up with plenty of time to put together a strong presentation.
