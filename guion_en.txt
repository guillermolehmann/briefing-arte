At five in the afternoon, in Chelsea, two doors open almost at the same hour, a few blocks apart from each other. One leads to the first New York show by the Brazilian artist Anna Maria Maiolino, sixty years of work hung for the occasion across both locations of the Swiss gallery Hauser and Wirth. The other leads to a darkened room at the New York gallery Lelong, where the Chilean conceptual artist Alfredo Jaar enlarged and re-projected the animation he made almost forty years ago for an electronic billboard in Times Square.

Good Thursday, Virginia, it is the tenth of September, and Chelsea gets today the second wave of the fall opening avalanche that started Tuesday across the rest of the city, with more than one hundred sixty new shows in one night. Jaar's piece is called "A Logo for America" and it dates back to nineteen eighty seven, when he made it for that same Times Square billboard as a critique of the word America being used to name only the United States, leaving the rest of the continent out. He is showing it again right as Jaar takes part this year in the Venice Biennale, the international art exhibition held every two years. Maiolino, who was part of the Brazilian Neo Concrete movement of the nineteen fifties, the movement that had the viewer activate the artwork with their own body instead of just looking at it, arrives at Hauser and Wirth with prints from the sixties and seventies, photography from the eighties, and sculpture from recent years, a full survey rather than a narrow slice. A couple of blocks away, Danh Vo, the Vietnamese artist, was already hanging in the British gallery White Cube as of yesterday after the preview, and the Museum of Modern Art closes the show pairing Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera the day after tomorrow, Saturday. On top of that, today also kicks off the fall edition of Asia Week, ten days of auction houses and galleries showing Asian art across the city.

On the market side, the map for October's fair is already taking shape. Frieze London confirmed its lineup for the fourteenth through the eighteenth, one hundred seventy one galleries from forty two countries, the same week Christie's sells the restituted Kandinsky we talked about yesterday. Among the confirmed names is Mendes Wood, the Brazilian gallery that, besides São Paulo, also has a space right here in New York, one more sign that interest in Brazilian names has not cooled off after Amaral nearly doubled its estimate last week and Maiolino opens in Chelsea this very day. And behind all of it, this whole season starts with the three big houses closing out twenty twenty five having sold fourteen billion one hundred million dollars between them, ten percent more than the year before. If November keeps that pace, the Blaquier consignment we have been following since August arrives with the wind at its back to support those estimates north of one hundred million dollars a piece.

Today's move, for clients who follow Brazilian art or Latin American conceptual work, send them tonight the double opening of Maiolino and Jaar in Chelsea, with a concrete invitation to meet there between five and eight. It is the kind of invite that needs no other excuse than wanting to see something new.

Tomorrow we pick up whatever these two openings leave behind, along with whatever the second half of the fall avalanche brings across the rest of the city.
