With Greater New York closing tomorrow, those three shows are what August has left until September gets underway.

On the market side, this week's news came from Phillips itself. The house closed its May Evening Sale of modern and contemporary art in New York with all forty one lots on offer finding buyers, what the trade calls a white glove sale, and the total reached one hundred and fifteen million two hundred and twenty thousand dollars, more than double what that same sale had done a year earlier. Among the priciest lots were a Warhol, sixteen repeated portraits of Jackie Kennedy, which went for sixteen million two hundred and twenty five thousand dollars, and a small untitled Pollock that reached nine million one hundred and sixty eight thousand. That same week, Christie's auctioned the full collection of a New York collector alongside its twentieth century sale, and together the two brought in one billion one hundred and twenty one million dollars, while Sotheby's gathered four hundred and thirty three million across its own sales in the same stretch. The scale gap we have been tracking in the course between Phillips and the other two houses shows up again, this time in the postwar and contemporary category instead of the moderns, though that same week also made clear that Phillips can sell a Warhol or a Pollock and still walk away with a fully sold room, something neither of the other two managed in their own sales those days.

Today's move is to write to two or three clients who buy postwar or pop American art to let them know that Phillips' May sale went white glove and more than doubled last year's result, because that is the kind of fact that supports a conversation about consigning something of their own before the next Evening Sale, already dated for the eighteenth of November. It is worth getting ahead of it, because between the big November sales in New York and Miami Art Week, which opens December first, everyone's calendar fills up fast and there is no easy moment left to sit down and look at a consignment calmly.
