Now the big story of the week. The hidden painting is from nineteen twenty, by the Dutch painter Piet Mondrian, the one behind the grids of black lines and primary colors that became a symbol of modern art, and it had never come to auction because it spent the last twenty five years locked away in a private collection where nobody saw it. This week it starts its pre-sale tour, and from Thursday the seventeenth through Sunday the twentieth of September you can see it for free at Christie's here in New York, before it moves on to London, where it goes under the hammer on the fourteenth of October with an estimate of twenty to thirty million pounds, roughly twenty seven to forty million dollars. That night in London it won't be alone. It's the same evening sale, during Frieze week, where Christie's had already confirmed the restituted Kandinsky we told you about yesterday, so the house is building one of the strongest nights of the year around two paintings whose stories speak for themselves, the one the Nazis forced into auction and the one nobody saw for a quarter century.
