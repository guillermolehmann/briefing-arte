"The Armory Show has always reflected New York's openness to artists, galleries, and ideas from across the Americas and the wider world." That is what the fair's director, Kyla McMillan, said this week while confirming the final lineup for this year's edition, two hundred and thirty galleries with both of its curated sections looking entirely toward the Caribbean and its diaspora.

Tonight a handful of small shows open at a couple of downtown galleries, among them AIR and Kaje, the last light round before next week brings more than two hundred and eighty new exhibitions across the city all at once. Good Saturday morning, Virginia, today is the fifth of September, and you still have a relatively quiet weekend before the calendar floods over. Two days remain for Botero in New York, the show Sotheby's is running out of the building that used to be the old Breuer home, with more than twenty paintings and sculptures by the Colombian artist Fernando Botero, it closes Monday for the Labor Day holiday, and it is worth going this weekend because after that it will be gone. Tuesday the ninth White Cube welcomes Danh Vo back with a preview, and Thursday the tenth Hauser and Wirth reopens both its Chelsea locations with the Brazilian artist Anna Maria Maiolino's first New York show, while MoMA still holds Frida and Diego, the exhibition on Kahlo and Rivera, through the twelfth, and the painter Minnie Evans's retrospective remains open at the Whitney through January, worth folding into the same weekend rounds.

On the market side, keep an eye on what Frieze Seoul and Kiaf left behind this week, the two South Korean fairs that closed their editions with cautious but real sales. Hauser and Wirth sold a three part work by the American artist Mark Bradford for four and a half million dollars, and a nineteen seventy one painting by the Korean artist Yoo Youngkuk went for somewhere between one million six hundred thousand and one million eight hundred thousand dollars, a record for a Korean artist at that fair since it launched in twenty twenty two. It works as an early read on the Armory Show, which on top of its two hundred and thirty confirmed galleries expanded its Presents and Solo sections to fifty five and twenty five galleries respectively, and which for the first time in its thirty years will hand out two museum acquisition prizes. The fair also reworked its floor plan, separating exhibitors from the Americas and the Caribbean from the block anchored by New York galleries, worth keeping in mind before walking it with a client. It adds to the momentum you have already been tracking in Latin American art, Sotheby's has sold one hundred and twenty nine million dollars in the category since twenty twenty four, with the Colombian artist Olga de Amaral's record, three million one hundred and twenty five thousand dollars this past November, standing as the most recent and highest reference point in the group.

Today's move is to message two or three clients who have been asking you about Botero in New York, telling them this is the last real weekend to see it before it closes Monday, and while you are at it, ask them whether they already have a Latin American name in mind to see up close once the Armory Show opens at the end of the month.

That closes out the weekend's agenda. Next Monday there is no more room for Botero, and the rest of September fills up with nothing but new names.
