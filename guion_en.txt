How many years has it been since Danh Vo last showed new work in New York? Eight, since the retrospective the Guggenheim gave him in two thousand eighteen, titled Take My Breath Away. That silence breaks on September ninth, when White Cube, the London gallery with a New York outpost, opens a show dedicated to the Danish artist of Vietnamese origin, with a preview that night and the formal opening the next day. Vo builds sculptures out of fragments of antiques and religious objects, and this return is good news for the gallery, which lost all three of its New York directors this spring, because it confirms the fall program is still moving ahead despite the leadership turnover.

Good morning, Virginia, today is Thursday the thirteenth of August, and even though summer still hangs heavy over the city, there is plenty to cover before the weekend.

On the agenda, four days remain for Greater New York at PS1, which closes Sunday the seventeenth. It is the biggest free show of the New York summer, with more than fifty young artists spread across the whole building, staged this year as part of the museum's fiftieth anniversary. The Whitney Biennial stays open through the twenty third, and that Botero show you already know, at Sotheby's Breuer building, runs through September seventh, with work available for direct purchase right there. And if you swing by Chelsea tonight, Yancey Richardson gallery opens "Once More, With Feeling," a photography show featuring this year's Yale MFA graduates, a cheap and quick way to see what the next generation of young galleries is watching before those names start showing up in pricier catalogs.

On to the market. The Armory Show, New York's main contemporary art fair in September, locked in fixed dates, the twenty fourth through the twenty seventh, at the Javits Center, with more galleries than last year and its curated sections, Presents and Solo, expanded by more than twenty percent. This edition also puts special focus on Caribbean artists and their diaspora within those sections. Among the confirmed galleries is Praxis, based in Buenos Aires, worth keeping in mind if a client asks what Latin American presence the fair will have this year. With the date locked in, the season begins where the calls that matter are the ones coordinating visits ahead of time and finding out what each gallery is bringing, instead of deciding on the spot in front of the work. It is also the first major gathering of the season after the August lull, so it usually works as the earliest read on how the fall is shaping up ahead of the November sales.

Today's move is to let two or three clients who follow White Cube know that Danh Vo is coming back to New York after eight years, tell them the preview is September ninth, and suggest going together before Armory week hits and their calendars fill up on their own.
