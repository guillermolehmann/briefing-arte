On the market side, this week's big story was not a sale result but a consignment. Sotheby's announced it secured the collection of the Blaquier family, one of Argentina's most prominent, for its November sale in New York. The paintings include Van Gogh, Cézanne, Degas, Renoir and Pissarro, valued together at more than four hundred and fifty million dollars, with the Van Gogh alone, a chestnut tree in blossom painted in eighteen ninety, estimated between one hundred and fifty and two hundred million. The family had a dispute with Argentine customs in two thousand twenty three over a dozen of those paintings leaving for Luxembourg, and just last month the agency dropped the fine, which cleared the way for the consignment. It is the kind of collection that reshapes the entire November calendar, and it connects directly to what had already been happening earlier this year, because back in May, in the same modern art category, Sotheby's sold three hundred and three million dollars in a single night, led by a Matisse that had not appeared on the market in almost fifty years and went for forty eight million four hundred thousand dollars.

Today's move is to let clients who follow the Argentine circuit closely know that the Blaquier collection is heading to auction in November at Sotheby's, even though the sale is not at Phillips.
