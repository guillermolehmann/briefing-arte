The art calendar didn't wait for summer to end here. On the other side of the world the fall season already opened, with Frieze Seoul and the twenty-fifth edition of Kiaf Seoul bringing together one hundred and seventy-five galleries this week at the COEX convention center in the South Korean capital. New York is still catching its breath at the end of August, but today, Wednesday the second of September, the countdown is already running on several fronts at once, so it's worth mapping out what's closing and what's opening before it all lands at the same time.

What closes first is Botero in New York, at Sotheby's space in the old Breuer building, more than twenty paintings and sculptures from the period the Colombian artist Fernando Botero spent living here, between nineteen sixty and nineteen seventy three, with part of the works still available for private purchase alongside the show. It closes next Monday, which is also the Labor Day holiday, so it's better not to leave it for the long weekend. MoMA is holding Frida and Diego, The Last Dream through the twelfth, the show pairing Mexican painter Frida Kahlo with her husband Diego Rivera, also a Mexican painter. And the Whitney keeps its retrospective of Minnie Evans, a self-taught African American painter the museum hadn't shown solo in fifty years, running through January.

What opens comes right on its heels, almost every other day. Next Tuesday, the ninth of September, the gallery White Cube welcomes back the artist Danh Vo, who hasn't shown in the city since his two thousand eighteen Guggenheim retrospective, with a preview before opening to the public the following day. And that same Thursday the tenth, the gallery Hauser and Wirth reopens both its Chelsea locations after the summer break, with the first New York show for Italian Brazilian artist Anna Maria Maiolino among the three exhibitions it's launching. All of that builds toward the Armory Show, opening September twenty fourth at the Javits Center, and this week it added a detail that matters directly to your Latin American book of clients, the Platform section, dedicated to larger scale installations within the fair, is titled this year Passages and Thresholds and is curated by Jovanna Venegas of SculptureCenter, focused on how space gets built and rebuilt across the Caribbean. Confirmed artists already include María Magdalena Campos Pons, Hew Locke, and Bony Ramirez.

On the market side, that same section points to something broader, the fair has been shifting the geographic focus of its curated sections toward the Caribbean and its diaspora ever since it announced its full exhibitor list in July, and it's now adding two museum acquisition prizes, new this year, one for the Pérez Art Museum Miami and one for the Bronx Museum. For an advisor with a Latin American book, that curatorial choice works as the fair's way of telling a buyer from the region where to look first the moment they walk into the hall.

Today's move ties together what's closing with what's opening. Write to two or three clients who haven't been through Botero in New York yet, letting them know it closes Monday, and while you're at it tell them next Tuesday brings the preview of Danh Vo at White Cube, so they can build one single Chelsea outing before Hauser and Wirth joins in the following Thursday and, two weeks after that, the Armory Show takes over their whole end of month calendar.
