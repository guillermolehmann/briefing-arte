August empties out the New York galleries more than any other month, and this week proves it, with barely any new openings while the city waits for September to come back. There are still a couple of hard deadlines before fall really begins, though. Good morning, Virginia, today is Friday, the fourteenth of August. Greater New York, the show taking over the entire historic PS1 building in Queens, which this year also marks the institution's fiftieth anniversary with more than fifty artists from across the city, closes this Sunday, so you have only two days left to see it. The Whitney Biennial runs through the twenty third, and over at the Breuer building, the venue Sotheby's now uses for exhibitions, Botero in New York stays open through the seventh of September, more than twenty works from the Colombian painter's New York years, painted between nineteen sixty and nineteen seventy three, with the new detail that some of those pieces are also available for private sale, not just on view.

There is fresh news at White Cube on the topic that had been hanging since the director shake up earlier this month. Beyond Danh Vo's return on the ninth and tenth of September, the gallery confirmed that in November it presents a new installation by Doris Salcedo, the Colombian artist known for objects and materials that speak to her country's political violence, in her first major New York show since the retrospective that opened in Chicago in two thousand fifteen and later traveled to the Guggenheim. The new work relates to Uprooted, the installation she debuted at the Sharjah Biennial in two thousand twenty three, and it stays on view at White Cube through January.

On the market side, people are still digesting what Frieze New York left behind in May for Latin American art. Fourteen galleries from the region took part, eight of them Brazilian, and one of the most talked about stories is Mitre Galeria, from São Paulo, which moved up from the younger galleries section into the fair's main roster. Its founder said that three years ago seventy percent of his clients lived in Brazil, and today the split is about even between local and international collectors, a sign that international interest in Brazilian art keeps growing well beyond the numbers Sotheby's already built in the category, where it has racked up a hundred and twenty nine million dollars since two thousand twenty four.

Today's move is to let two or three clients who follow the Latin American circuit know that Doris Salcedo opens at White Cube in November, before the fall calendar fills up with the November sales and Miami Art Week. A short message does it, telling them you will watch for the exact date and let them know as soon as the invitation list opens, no ask attached yet.
