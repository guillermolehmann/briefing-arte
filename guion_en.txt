What do you do when a museum doesn't close a show on one single day, but floor by floor instead? That's exactly what's happening right now with the Whitney Biennial. Good morning, Virginia, today is Wednesday, the nineteenth of August. The fifth floor of the show closes this Sunday, the twenty third, just as we'd been counting down, but the first floor stays open through the seventh of September, and the sixth floor runs all the way to the twelfth of October. The countdown we'd been following through yesterday only covered part of the Biennial, so if any of your clients already wrote it off as closed, let them know there are still two floors left to see.

The rest of today's New York agenda. Botero in New York is still on view at Sotheby's Breuer building, with part of the works available for private sale, through the seventh of September. Photographer Sim Chi Yin's installation at Storefront for Art and Architecture, about the mass deportation of the Chinese community from Malaysia in the middle of last century, runs through the twenty ninth of August. MoMA keeps Frida and Diego, The Last Dream, with Kahlo and Rivera, open through the twelfth of September. Today is also the last day of the Free Art Book Fair at Flag Art Foundation, a small Chelsea foundation devoted to artist publications, so if that circuit interests you, this is your last chance. And tonight, from six to eight, Parent Company, a new downtown gallery, opens Wave at Night, by the duo Blinn and Lambert, two Brooklyn artists who've spent the last ten years working with video and optical animation.

On the market side, a small data point worth watching. Back in May, at Phillips' Evening Sale, a work by Kikuo Saito titled Cerise sold for two hundred and forty five thousand one hundred dollars, an artist record. Saito rarely shows up in a headline next to Pollock or Basquiat, and yet that result matters, because it lines up with what we told you a week ago, that this half's recovery wasn't only about the marquee names' evening sales, day sales improved too, and a fresh mid-tier record like this one is that improvement showing up in the flesh.

Today's other piece of news is that the Armory Show confirmed its full exhibitor list for its September edition, running the twenty fourth through the twenty seventh at the Javits Center. Two hundred and thirty galleries from more than thirty countries, with the Solo section, the one that gives each booth to a single artist, growing by twenty percent, and Brazil's Baró Galeria presenting a full solo booth of Ayako Rokkaku there. This year's curated sections focus on the Caribbean and its diaspora, and Argentina's Praxis, from Buenos Aires, already confirmed weeks ago, is showing in the main section.

Today's play is simple. The full list of two hundred and thirty galleries is already online. Pick four or five that might interest your Latin American clients, using the Caribbean focus and the presence of Praxis and Baró as your reason to reach out, and send them a curated selection before September's calendar fills up. Showing up with the work already done is worth a lot more than sending the bare link and leaving each client to get lost among two hundred and thirty booths.

And before you start your day, today's lesson of Selling Art in New York is waiting for you, the one on Latin American concrete and kinetic artists, which ties straight back into the Biennial, into Sotheby's, and into everything you just heard on today's agenda.
