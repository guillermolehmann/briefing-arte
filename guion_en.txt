The whole autumn art calendar finally locked its dates this week. Frieze London set theirs, Sotheby's confirmed its own for Latin American art, and even the most accessible fair of the year now knows when it opens its doors. The week begins, this Monday, with a light agenda, the calm after the wave of openings Chelsea had last week.

In Chinatown, the small gallery Shin Gallery opens today a two-artist show pairing two Italian abstractionists, Lucio Fontana, the one who stopped painting on the canvas and started cutting into it with a blade, and Carla Prina, a peer of his generation left out of the history books, now getting her largest show in the country here, up through the twenty third of October. Nearby, today is the last day of the preview for Doyle's Asian art auction, running from noon to five in the afternoon, with a free tour at ten in the morning led by one of the house's specialists, ahead of the sale itself, which opens the day after tomorrow, Wednesday, with Chinese porcelain and bronzes and continues Thursday with Japanese works and pieces from the rest of Asia. And this week also brings the Affordable Art Fair, the fair for smaller works priced from one hundred dollars up to twelve thousand, opening Wednesday at the Starrett Lehigh Building in Chelsea with ninety one galleries from fourteen countries, a light counterpoint to this season's nine figure hammer prices.

On the market side, two new dates worth marking down. Sotheby's confirmed that its next dedicated Latin American art auction in New York lands on the seventh of October at eleven in the morning, the first firm date since the house passed one hundred and twenty nine million dollars sold in the category since two thousand twenty four, with more than twenty artist records along the way. And Frieze London, the British edition of the fair we already saw this year in New York and in Seoul, confirmed it runs from the fourteenth through the eighteenth of October, with the invitation only preview day landing on that same Wednesday the fourteenth, the very day Christie's holds its London sale of the Mondrian hidden away for twenty five years and the restituted Kandinsky we have been following. Three hundred galleries from forty eight countries will be there that week, so anyone traveling in for the sale finds the fair sitting on the same calendar, no need for two separate trips. It is the same logic we already use with New York and Miami, sale and fair pinned together so the trip pays off twice.

Today's move is a calendar one, getting ahead before November fills up all at once. Tell your Latin American clients about the seventh of October now, so they have Sotheby's dedicated sale blocked off with time to spare. And for anyone thinking about a London trip for the Mondrian or the Kandinsky, let them know Frieze runs that same week, the fourteenth through the eighteenth of October, so they can build one trip instead of two.

And today's lesson from Selling Art in New York is waiting for you, like every day, on the other show.
