August lives up to its reputation as a slow month, nearly every gallery in New York closed and the big auction houses still holding off on their fall calendar, except for one exception that keeps growing, the Blaquier collection consignment at Sotheby's. That consignment is this morning's fresh news, so that's where we start after the week's quick recap. Good morning, Virginia, it's Wednesday, the twenty sixth of August.

Before getting there, this week's agenda. Sim Chi Yin, the photographer whose installation at Storefront for Art and Architecture deals with the mass deportation of the Chinese community from Malaysia in the middle of last century, closes Saturday, so three days remain if you haven't caught it yet. Botero in New York, the show bringing together more than twenty paintings and sculptures from Fernando Botero's New York period, staged at Sotheby's Breuer building, stays open through the seventh of September, with some of the works also available for private sale alongside the exhibition. MoMA continues with Frida and Diego, The Last Dream, the show bringing together Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera, through the twelfth of September, and at the Whitney, the Minnie Evans retrospective, the museum's first dedicated to the artist in fifty years, with more than one hundred drawings and paintings organized together with the High Museum in Atlanta, stays up through January. September is packed with dates, opening with Danh Vo's return to White Cube, a preview on Wednesday the ninth from six to eight in the evening and a public opening the next day, and two and a half weeks later the Armory Show opens at the Javits Center, two hundred and thirty galleries with a special focus on the Caribbean and its diaspora.

The market overall is about as quiet as you'd expect at this point in the year, with most of the big houses still not moving on their fall catalogue. The exception is still the Blaquier collection, which added one more piece to the puzzle this week. The dispute the family had been dragging with Argentine customs since two thousand twenty three, which had held up the export of more than a dozen works, was finally resolved last month, and now it's been confirmed that resolution clears exactly those held pieces to join the November sale as well. The collection's total estimate remains above four hundred and fifty million dollars, with the Van Gogh estimated between one hundred and fifty and two hundred million and the Cézanne above one hundred and twenty, both already known, and from here the final catalogue could end up bigger than expected two weeks ago.

Today's move comes straight from that news. Write to two or three clients in the Argentine circuit you told about the Blaquier consignment last month, letting them know the customs dispute is now fully resolved and that the November sale is going to bring more works than expected, as a reason to pick the conversation back up before the catalogue closes for good.
