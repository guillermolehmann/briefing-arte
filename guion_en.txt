By this time tomorrow, the Museum of Modern Art will have taken Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera down off the wall. Frida and Diego, the Last Dream, the show the museum built together with the Met Opera, with new stage design layered over the two Mexican artists' paintings, closes this Saturday, so today is the last full day to catch it. Good Friday morning, Virginia, it's the eleventh of September, and the city's calendar is still as packed as it's been all week.

In Chelsea, a new show opens this afternoon at Luhring Augustine, the gallery on West Twenty Fourth Street, with four photographers who portray people without flattering them, among them Janice Guy and David Seidner, and it stays up through late October. Two blocks over, Danh Vo is still hanging at White Cube, along with yesterday's double opening at Hauser and Wirth and at Lelong gallery, so you can still put together a walk that hits all four stops. And today also kicks off the autumn edition of Asia Week, ten days in which auction houses and galleries across the city show ceramics, paintings, and objects from Asia, with the New York house Doyle opening its Asian art preview today and running through Monday, ahead of a sale split over two days, the sixteenth and seventeenth of September. All of this lands two and a half weeks before the Armory Show opens, so this afternoon's Chelsea agenda and these ten days of Asia Week are, in effect, the warm up before the big fair.

On the market side, The Art Newspaper ran a piece yesterday worth keeping handy for any conversation with a younger client. According to ArtTactic's report on the first half of the year, a third of the value the big three houses sold came from entire single owner collections, mostly Impressionist and Modern art, while on the other side of the market an auction house specializing in collectibles called Goldin sold a Pokémon card in pristine condition for sixteen million five hundred thousand dollars in February, and in June the auction house Heritage Auctions sold a sealed copy of the video game Super Mario Brothers for three million dollars, a record for that object. The report itself asks where the next generation's collecting is heading, toward the card or toward the painting, and it describes a traditional art market answering, for now, with more caution, galleries cutting costs and buyers choosing fewer names but safer ones. Against that uncertainty, the Latin American portfolio Virginia has been building stands on firmer ground, one hundred and twenty nine million dollars sold at Sotheby's since twenty twenty four and Olga de Amaral's auction record backed by six more sales spread across several houses, a multi year track record you can already show with numbers.

Today's move has two parts. For clients who asked about Frida and Diego, write to them right now that today is the last full day to go, because tomorrow it closes. And for the ones who follow photography or portraiture, add this afternoon's opening at Luhring Augustine as a light excuse to meet up in Chelsea before the Armory Show fills the calendar.
