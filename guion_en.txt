August is winding down across New York's galleries, and while the city empties out little by little, behind that quiet the biggest pieces of the season starting in September are falling into place. Good morning, Virginia, today is Thursday, the twentieth of August, and there are only a few days left of this lull before the calendar fills up all at once.

On the agenda, the fifth floor of the Whitney Biennial closes this Sunday, so there are only three days left to see it before it goes dark for good, though the first floor stays open through the seventh of September and the sixth floor runs through the twelfth of October. Botero in New York, the show Sotheby's is running at its Breuer building with twenty five works from the artist's New York period, remains open through the seventh of September, with part of the works already available for private sale alongside the exhibition. Photographer Sim Chi Yin's installation continues at Storefront for Art and Architecture through the twenty ninth, and MoMA keeps its show pairing Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera open through the twelfth of September. And inside the auction houses there's movement of people too, not just of artworks. Earlier this month Sotheby's brought on Natasha Le Bel, until then Christie's global head of communications, to take the same role at the rival house, a hire landing just before the most important season of the year kicks off, the same pattern of moves between the big houses you saw this spring when White Cube's three New York directors all left.

On the market side, the story that keeps growing is the Blaquier collection consignment, the Argentine family whose Impressionist and Post-Impressionist collection Sotheby's is selling in November, worth more than four hundred and fifty million dollars across Van Gogh, Cézanne, Degas, Renoir and Pissarro. The customs dispute that had held up a dozen of those works from leaving Argentina is now resolved, and word is that at least two of the collection's paintings, still unidentified publicly, are heading to sale with estimates above one hundred million dollars each, making them two of the most important lots of the entire fall season in New York. On the Latin American side specifically, the tally still holds up well. Sotheby's has now sold one hundred twenty nine million dollars in the category since two thousand twenty four, with more than twenty artist records set since two thousand seventeen, the same wave of collecting the course has been tracking week after week.
