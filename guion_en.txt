How much did anyone ever pay at auction for the priciest work by Anna Maria Maiolino, the artist born in Italy who grew up between Venezuela and Brazil and now shows across half the world? Seventy six thousand two hundred dollars. That is a small number for the institutional weight she carries, and it matters today, Tuesday, September first, with Thursday the tenth coming up fast, the day Hauser and Wirth reopens its two Chelsea locations after the summer with three shows at once, one of them the gallery's first New York exhibition of Maiolino, spanning six decades of work in ceramics, drawing, and sculpture built around the body and memory.

This week's calendar stays tight. Six days remain for Botero in New York at Sotheby's Breuer building, closing next Monday, more than twenty paintings and sculptures from the artist's New York period, part of it still available for private sale. MoMA keeps Frida and Diego, the show pairing Frida Kahlo with her husband Diego Rivera, open through the twelfth, and the Whitney's retrospective of painter Minnie Evans runs through January. Thursday the fourth unofficially opens Chelsea's fall season, with Hauser and Wirth's reopening as one of the week's strongest early moves, since that same Thursday the tenth the gallery also opens two more shows at its twenty second street location. Tuesday the ninth brings artist Danh Vo back to the city with a preview at White Cube before it opens to the public the following day. All of it builds toward the Armory Show, opening September twenty fourth at the Javits Center with roughly two hundred and thirty galleries from more than thirty countries, a Caribbean and diaspora focus, and two museum acquisition prizes handed out for the first time in the fair's more than thirty year history.

On the market side, Sotheby's has already folded contemporary Latin American art into its Contemporary Curated sales in New York, instead of leaving it isolated in a department that only gets activated once or twice a year. The next of those sales falls on October seventh, barely two weeks after the Armory Show closes, so buyers still riding the energy of the fair get a brand new catalogue to look through. That runs alongside the standalone biannual Latin American art sale, which comes out in May and November, and this November also shares its week with the consignment of the Argentine Blaquier collection, valued at more than four hundred and fifty million dollars. For you, building the client base you are building, that means at least three separate windows inside two months where a Latin American buyer can land on Sotheby's radar, not one appointment a year.

Today's move comes out of Hauser and Wirth's reopening. Write to two or three clients who follow postwar Latin American women artists, or who already asked you about Brazilian names, letting them know that Thursday the tenth brings the first New York show of Anna Maria Maiolino at the gallery, open reception from five to eight in the evening on eighteenth street. While you are at it, mention that her auction history is still thin, seventy six thousand two hundred dollars at the ceiling, so getting there now, before the market reacts to a show this size at a gallery this size, is not the same as getting there after it already has.
