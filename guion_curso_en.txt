What did Phillips' Evening Sale of May the nineteenth achieve, and why is a sale like that called white glove?............ It sold one hundred percent of the lots and one hundred percent of the value on offer, one hundred fifteen million two hundred sixteen thousand seven hundred dollars, more than double the year before, and the hammer result landed inside the pre-sale estimate range. It matters because a fully sold room shows demand spread across the whole catalogue, not just in the priciest lot.

What does the sequence of Fernando Botero sales show after his two thousand twenty three record?............ After the five million one hundred thirty two thousand dollar record set by The Musicians, Christie's placed five more paintings by the artist between two thousand twenty four and two thousand twenty five, all between six hundred ninety three thousand and one million seven hundred forty four thousand dollars. That repetition is what separates an isolated record from a market with real depth, and that commercial momentum lines up this season with two institutional signals, the show Sotheby's is devoting to him at its Breuer building and MoMA's exhibition on Frida Kahlo and Diego Rivera, both keeping New York's interest in Mexican modernism visible, the tradition Botero commercially inherits.

When was Frida Kahlo's record set, and what price did it reach?............ On the twentieth of November, two thousand twenty five, at Sotheby's New York, El sueño, the bed, sold for fifty four million six hundred sixty thousand dollars, the highest price ever paid for a work by a woman artist at auction. The work led a session devoted to Surrealist art, and its presence pulled buyers and press toward the rest of that night's catalogue, the same anchor mechanism you already saw with Picasso and Matisse in the week's first lesson.

What separates Brazilian Neo-Concretism from Venezuelan Kinetic art?............ Neo-Concretism, with Lygia Clark and Hélio Oiticica, activates the work through the viewer's body, touching it or putting it on, while Kinetic art, with Jesús Rafael Soto and Carlos Cruz-Diez, activates it through the eye, changing as the viewer's angle or position shifts. Lygia Clark's Bichos are hinged metal plates the viewer bends by hand, while a Soto mobile changes appearance depending on the angle it's viewed from, without anyone touching it. Mixing up the two in front of a client is this lesson's most common mistake.

What price pattern does Derek Fordjour show that Firelei Báez doesn't show yet?............ Fordjour sold Numbers for eight hundred eighty nine thousand dollars at Phillips in May two thousand twenty four, and Twelve Tribes for one million one hundred forty three thousand dollars that November, twenty percent above his previous record at Christie's, two different houses confirming the same upward direction two years apart. Báez's record at Phillips, by contrast, was beaten that same day by Christie's, with no second sale yet to confirm it holds. The repetition over time is what separates a real track record from a single strong afternoon.

What happened with Basquiat at Phillips in two thousand twenty four, and what happened to that result two years later?............ Phillips sold Untitled, ELMAR, for forty six million four hundred seventy nine thousand dollars in May two thousand twenty four, but in May two thousand twenty six Sotheby's beat that figure with fifty two million seven hundred eighteen thousand dollars for another work by the same artist. Phillips' result was an isolated extraordinary case, not a sustained edge over the other houses, the house with the highest and most recent result isn't necessarily the one with the strongest structural access to that artist.

Among the records for Diego Rivera, Rufino Tamayo, Wifredo Lam and Roberto Matta, what do they have in common, besides all being old?............ None of the four has recent public evidence of repeated sales confirming a current price trend, unlike Botero, and their records sit far back in time, Rivera since two thousand twenty two at fourteen million one hundred thirty thousand dollars, Tamayo since two thousand eight at seven million two hundred nine thousand, Lam since two thousand twenty at nine million six hundred three thousand eight hundred, and Matta since two thousand twelve at five million ten thousand five hundred. Talking about those four names requires leaning on the specific work being evaluated, not the artist's historical record.

Why do two Remedios Varo records, at two different houses and in two different mediums, matter more than one alone?............ Because her overall record, six million one hundred eighty six thousand eight hundred dollars at Sotheby's, was reinforced in two thousand twenty six by a work-on-paper record of four million four hundred sixty seven thousand dollars at Christie's. Two different houses confirming value, in two different categories, is the kind of sustained-demand evidence a single isolated record can't provide on its own.

A client tells you that since Diego Rivera holds a fourteen million one hundred thirty thousand dollar record, any work of his has to keep rising in value. What do you tell him?............ That the record certifies one exceptional night, not sustained demand across the rest of his catalogue, and that without recent, comparable sales confirming it you have to appraise that specific work, not the name. It's the same mistake you already saw with Roberto Matta, whose two thousand twelve record sits alongside a recent sale well below earlier prices, and before talking numbers it's worth suggesting he authenticate the work with the artist's foundation or committee.

A client shows you a Gego sculpture next to a Soto mobile at a fair and says they're the same movement. What do you explain?............ That they share an era and a region but not a mechanism, because Gego works with suspended wire structures and carries institutional prestige comparable to Olga de Amaral's, while Soto activates the work through the viewer's optical movement, and that Gego's resale record still hasn't shown the repetition Amaral's has. That's the difference between institutional validation, which both share equally, and market depth, which was this whole week's central theme.
